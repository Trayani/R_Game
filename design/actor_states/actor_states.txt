Actors have following states


1) NO SUBCELL
Actor owns no subcell, that would be primary subcell for him.
In this state, the actor can receive destination for movement, but cannot move until a primary subcell is reserved.

EVERY FRAME:  the actor tries to reserve a nearby subcell as a primary cell.
This is limited to the four subcells whose centers form a rectangle around the actor's float-precise position.
If more subcells are available, prioritize the closest one.
If the actor was unable to reserve a subcell and is very close to the rectangle boundary (2px, but configurable) ,
he can also try to reserve the neighboring rectangle's subcells (2 of which are shared by actor's rectangle and the neighbour
in the close-by boundary). This applies both vertically, and horizontally.
if the actor successfully reserved a subcell, he enters the PSC_ALIGNMENT state

2) PRIMARY SUBCELL ALIGNMENT "PSC_ALIGNMENT"
In this state, the actor has to place himself in the center on his primary subcell.
EVERY FRAME: actor moves DIRECTLY to the subcell center until it is reached.
A specific destination CAN be set to the actor, but he ignores it and moves to PSC center first.

3) IDLE
EVERY FRAME: if no destination is set, do nothing.
Otherwise:  actor calculates the movement direction and target according to [actor_directing.txt] design file
- if no direction was determined, try again in the next frame
- if direction WAS determined, enter MOVE state

4) MOVE
EVERY FRAME: actor moves to the target position.
Once the target has been reached, it depends
- the target was a subcell center (was normalized): actor sets his primary cell to the target subcell and enters IDLE. If the
target was the same as destination, unassign the destination value (the actor reached destination and should stay until new destination is set)
- the target was a rectangle boundary (not normalized): the boundary is defined by 2 subcells. The actor sets his
primary cell to the closer subcell and enters IDLE
