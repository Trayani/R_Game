SUBCELL TRIANGLES - DESTINATIONDIRECT MODE
Q&A SPECIFICATION DOCUMENT
==========================================

!! This specification is for reservation_mode = "DestinationDirect" !!

This document uses a Question & Answer format to precisely define the triangle-based
subcell navigation system. Each concept is presented as a question with a detailed answer,
examples, and implementation notes.


================================================================================
SECTION 1: CORE CONCEPTS
================================================================================

Q1.1: What is a Primary Subcell (PSC)?
---------------------------------------
A: The Primary Subcell (PSC) is the single subcell that represents an actor's current
   position in the grid. It is:
   - Always reserved by the actor (exclusive ownership)
   - Required before any other navigation can occur
   - Determined by which subcell contains the actor's float position center
   - Previously called "current" subcell in old implementation

   Implementation Note: Use terminology "primary" consistently in code, not "current".


Q1.2: What happens if an actor spawns without a PSC?
-----------------------------------------------------
A: The actor must immediately attempt to reserve a PSC in their supercell (the cell
   containing their spawn position). The process is:

   1. Identify supercell from actor's float position center
   2. Attempt to reserve the first available subcell in that supercell
   3. If ALL subcells are reserved: actor remains at exact spawn float position and
      waits, checking every tick for availability
   4. Once any subcell becomes available, reserve it as PSC

   Edge Case: This should only occur during grid initialization with high actor density.

   Testing Required: Create tests with overcrowded spawn areas to verify waiting behavior
   and proper PSC acquisition.


Q1.3: What is a Triangle in this system?
-----------------------------------------
A: A triangle is a movement corridor spanning multiple subcells. It is NOT confined to
   a single subcell. A triangle is defined by three subcell centers as vertices:

   Triangle Vertices:
   1. Primary Subcell (PSC) center - where actor currently is
   2. Reserved Subcell A center - on one cardinal direction (H or V)
   3. Reserved Subcell B center - on the perpendicular cardinal direction

   Example: For PSC at (*), with horizontal reservation at (*4) and diagonal at (*3),
            the triangle vertices are: (*, *3, *4)

   Visual:
         *3 (NE diagonal)
         /|
        / |
       /  |
      *---*4 (E horizontal)
    (PSC)

   The triangle interior is the region bounded by these three subcell centers.


Q1.4: What makes a triangle "optimal"?
---------------------------------------
A: A triangle is OPTIMAL if and only if:

   "Drawing a straight line from the actor's current position to the destination
    passes through the triangle's interior (or along its edges)."

   Mathematical Definition:
   - Let V = vector from actor position to destination
   - Let T = triangle formed by (PSC, SC_A, SC_B)
   - Optimal if: ray from actor along V intersects T before exiting

   Non-Optimal Triangle: Any triangle that does NOT contain the destination vector.
   The actor cannot move directly toward destination within a non-optimal triangle.

   Implementation: Use ray-triangle intersection test or point-in-triangle test
   on the clamped destination direction.


Q1.5: What is an H-triangle vs V-triangle?
-------------------------------------------
A: H and V triangles differ in which cardinal direction is prioritized:

   H-Triangle (Horizontally-sided):
   - One edge spans horizontally (connects horizontal reserved SC to PSC)
   - The perpendicular edge is vertical
   - Favors horizontal movement
   - Used when: |destination.x - actor.x| > |destination.y - actor.y|

   V-Triangle (Vertically-sided):
   - One edge spans vertically (connects vertical reserved SC to PSC)
   - The perpendicular edge is horizontal
   - Favors vertical movement
   - Used when: |destination.x - actor.x| <= |destination.y - actor.y|

   Classification Rule:
   ```
   if abs(dest.x - actor.x) > abs(dest.y - actor.y):
       use H-triangle
   else:
       use V-triangle
   ```


================================================================================
SECTION 2: RESERVATION LOGIC
================================================================================

Q2.1: What are the 8 neighboring subcells around PSC?
------------------------------------------------------
A: From any PSC (*), there are 8 adjacent subcell centers:

   *1 (NW diagonal)    *2 (N vertical)     *3 (NE diagonal)
   *8 (W horizontal)   * (PSC)             *4 (E horizontal)
   *7 (SW diagonal)    *6 (S vertical)     *5 (SE diagonal)

   Categories:
   - 2 Horizontal: *8 (W), *4 (E)
   - 2 Vertical: *2 (N), *6 (S)
   - 4 Diagonal: *1 (NW), *3 (NE), *5 (SE), *7 (SW)


Q2.2: What subcells must be reserved for diagonal movement?
------------------------------------------------------------
A: Diagonal movement requires reserving BOTH:
   1. The diagonal subcell (e.g., *3 for NE)
   2. One cardinal subcell (either horizontal *4 or vertical *2)

   The cardinal choice depends on H vs V classification (see Q1.5):
   - If H-triangle: reserve diagonal + horizontal cardinal
   - If V-triangle: reserve diagonal + vertical cardinal

   Example: Moving NE with H-preference
   - Reserve *3 (NE diagonal) AND *4 (E horizontal)
   - Forms triangle (*, *3, *4)


Q2.3: What is the reservation priority order?
----------------------------------------------
A: Reservation attempts follow a prioritized order based on destination direction.
   The actor tries each option until one succeeds (all SCs available and not blocked).

   General Priority Template:
   1st: Optimal triangle (contains destination vector)
   2nd: Alternative triangle (different H/V choice)
   3rd: Pure cardinal direction only
   4th: Pure perpendicular cardinal direction

   Specific Examples (see ASCII diagram in Q2.4 for context):

   D1(NEH) - NorthEast with Horizontal preference:
     1. (*4, *3) - H-triangle NE
     2. (*2, *3) - V-triangle NE  [if closer to dest than *4]
     2. *4       - pure horizontal
     3. *2       - pure vertical

   D2(E) - Pure East:
     1. *8 - pure horizontal east
     2. (*1, *2) - V-triangle NE [if closer to dest]
     2. (*7, *6) - V-triangle SE [if closer to dest]

   D5(SEV) - SouthEast with Vertical preference:
     1. (*5, *6) - V-triangle SE
     2. *6       - pure vertical
     2. (*4, *5) - H-triangle SE [if closer to dest than *6]
     3. *4       - pure horizontal

   Tiebreaker for "OR" cases: When multiple options have equal priority (e.g.,
   "(*1,*2) OR (*7,*6)"), choose the option whose subcell center is closer to
   the destination.

Q2.5: What is a "mirror triangle"?
-----------------------------------
A: A mirror triangle is the NEXT triangle in the sequence when continuing toward
   destination along an optimal path. It "mirrors" the current triangle by sharing
   two subcell vertices and adding one new subcell.

   Geometry: Current triangle + mirror triangle = rectangular corridor

   Example: Actor in triangle (*, *7, *6) moving toward destination DD(SWV)
   - Current triangle vertices: (*, *7, *6)
   - Mirror triangle vertices: (*A, *7, *6) where *A is next diagonal subcell
   - Together they form a rectangle with corners at (*, *6, *7, *A)

   Visual:
       *-----*6
       |\    |
       | \ M |  M = mirror triangle (*A, *7, *6)
       |  \  |
       | C \ |  C = current triangle (*, *7, *6)
       |    \|
       *7----*A

   Purpose: Allows continuous diagonal movement toward destination without
   zigzagging to subcell centers.


Q2.6: How does mirror triangle reservation work?
-------------------------------------------------
A: When an actor reaches a triangle boundary and wants to continue toward destination:

   Step 1: Identify which new subcell would form the mirror
           - Shared edge = the two vertices closest to destination
           - New subcell = the diagonal neighbor that completes the rectangle

   Step 2: Attempt to reserve the new subcell

   Step 3: If successful:
           - Add new subcell to reservations
           - Keep the 2 shared subcells reserved
           - Release the 1 old subcell no longer needed (based on release eagerness)

   Step 4: If reservation fails (blocked or reserved by another actor):
           - Find the reserved SC closest to destination
           - Use that as new "base" and follow standard reservation priority from Q2.3
           - This may result in moving to SC center rather than continuing on boundary

   Example from Q2.5:
   - Current reservations: (*, *7, *6)
   - Approaching boundary between *7 and *6
   - Reserve *A to form mirror (*A, *7, *6)
   - Release * (the PSC behind the actor)
   - New reservations: (*A, *7, *6) where *A is now the new PSC


Q2.7: How are mirror triangles discovered?
-------------------------------------------
A: Dynamically, one triangle at a time. There is NO pathfinding or precomputation
   of triangle chains.

   Process Each Tick:
   1. Actor knows current PSC
   2. Actor knows currently reserved subcells (if any)
   3. Actor evaluates destination direction
   4. Actor attempts reservation based on current position (see Q2.3)
   5. If in optimal triangle approaching boundary: attempt mirror reservation (Q2.6)

   Key Principle: "Organic Navigation"
   - Actor behavior depends ONLY on current position + destination + reserved SCs
   - Does NOT depend on historical path or previous triangles
   - Spawning actor at position P should produce identical behavior regardless of
     how they arrived at P

   Testing Approach: Spawn actors at many positions along an expected path. Verify
   that each spawned actor follows the same trajectory from that position forward.


================================================================================
SECTION 3: MOVEMENT BEHAVIOR
================================================================================

Q3.1: Where does the actor move each tick?
-------------------------------------------
A: Movement target depends on what is currently reserved:

   Case A: NO reservations beyond PSC
           Target = PSC center
           (Actor stays centered in their primary subcell)

   Case B: Pure cardinal reservation (H or V only)
           Target = reserved SC center
           Movement = direct line from current position to reserved SC center
           No triangle geometry involved

   Case C: Optimal triangle reservation (diagonal + cardinal)
           Target = destination position, clamped to triangle boundary
           Movement = toward destination, stop at triangle edge if needed

   Case D: Non-optimal triangle reservation
           Target = diagonal SC center
           Movement = toward diagonal SC center
           (Cannot move directly to destination since triangle doesn't contain it)

   Implementation: Each tick, calculate target position based on current reservations,
   then move actor toward target at configured movement speed.


Q3.2: How do you calculate target position for an optimal triangle?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A: For an optimal triangle, the target is the destination clamped to triangle bounds.

   Algorithm:
   1. Get actor's current float position (ax, ay)
   2. Get destination position (dx, dy)
   3. Create ray from (ax, ay) toward (dx, dy)
   4. Find intersection of ray with triangle boundary
      - If ray stays inside triangle: target = (dx, dy) [destination itself]
      - If ray exits triangle: target = intersection point on triangle edge

   Implementation Options:
   - Ray-triangle intersection test
   - Or check if destination is inside triangle; if yes use it, else project onto edges

   Example: Triangle (*, *3, *4), destination at D1(NEH)
   - If destination is beyond *3-*4 edge: target = point on edge closest to dest direction
   - If destination is inside triangle: target = destination exactly


Q3.3: What does "move toward target" mean precisely?
-----------------------------------------------------
A: Each tick, move the actor's float position toward the calculated target position.

   Movement Vector:
   ```
   direction = normalize(target - actor_position)
   displacement = direction * movement_speed * delta_time
   new_position = actor_position + displacement
   ```

   Clamping: If displacement would overshoot target, clamp to target exactly:
   ```
   distance_to_target = length(target - actor_position)
   if length(displacement) > distance_to_target:
       new_position = target
   ```

   Configuration: `movement_speed` should be a configurable parameter (e.g., 2.0 grid
   units per second).


Q3.4: What happens when actor reaches the target position?
-----------------------------------------------------------
A: Depends on reservation eagerness configuration (see Section 4):

   CENTER eagerness:
   - Actor reaches target exactly
   - THEN begins attempting next reservation
   - May pause briefly if next reservation isn't immediately available

   ROUND eagerness:
   - Actor begins attempting next reservation when target is closer than all other
     reserved SCs (see Q4.2)
   - Typically starts reserving before reaching target
   - Results in smoother continuous movement


Q3.5: When does the actor's PSC change?
----------------------------------------
A: The PSC changes when the actor's float position center enters a different subcell.

   Process:
   1. Each tick, check which subcell contains actor_position
   2. If different from current PSC:
      a. The new subcell must already be reserved by this actor
      b. Set new subcell as PSC
      c. Release old PSC (timing depends on release_eagerness - see Q4.3)

   Validation: It is an ERROR if actor's position enters a subcell that is:
   - Not reserved by this actor
   - Reserved by another actor
   - In a blocked cell

   This ensures collision-free movement through reservation system.


================================================================================
SECTION 4: TIMING & EAGERNESS CONFIGURATION
================================================================================

Q4.1: What is reservation_threshold_distance?
----------------------------------------------
A: A configuration parameter that controls when the actor starts attempting to reserve
   the next subcell(s).

   Definition: When actor is within this distance of the target position, begin
   attempting reservation for the next movement.

   Default Value: 0.1 grid units

   Purpose: Prevents gaps in movement where actor reaches target but has no next
   reservation yet. Allows for smooth continuous motion.

   Implementation:
   ```
   distance_to_target = length(target_position - actor_position)
   if distance_to_target <= reservation_threshold_distance:
       attempt_next_reservation()
   ```


Q4.2: What is reservation_eagerness and how does it work?
----------------------------------------------------------
A: Controls WHEN actor begins attempting next reservation.

   CENTER Mode:
   - Begin reserving after reaching target position
   - "Reached" = within reservation_threshold_distance of target
   - Conservative: only looks ahead one step

   ROUND Mode:
   - Begin reserving when target position is closer than all other reserved SCs
   - "Closer" = distance from actor's current float position
   - Formula: min(distance(actor_pos, reserved_SC[i])) > distance(actor_pos, target)
   - Aggressive: may reserve multiple steps ahead


Q4.3: What is release_eagerness and how does it work?
------------------------------------------------------
A: Controls WHEN actor releases subcells that are no longer needed.

   CENTER Mode:
   - Release subcell only after reaching the position where it becomes redundant

   For SC targets: Release old PSC after reaching new SC center
   For boundary targets: Release redundant SC after reaching boundary position

   ROUND Mode:
   - Release subcell when the release position is closer than the subcell being released
   - Formula: distance(actor_pos, release_position) < distance(actor_pos, redundant_SC)

   Example for mirror transition (*, *7, *6) -> (*A, *7, *6):
   - CENTER: Release * only after actor reaches boundary between *7 and *6
   - ROUND: Release * when actor is closer to that boundary than to * center


Q4.4: Why is ROUND reservation + CENTER release forbidden?
-----------------------------------------------------------
A: This combination causes excessive subcell reservation that degrades system performance.

   The Problem:
   - ROUND reservation: Actor reserves aggressively, potentially 2-3 triangles ahead
   - CENTER release: Actor holds onto old subcells until exactly reaching release point
   - Result: Actor simultaneously reserves 4-6 subcells unnecessarily

   Impact:
   - Blocks other actors from using subcells that current actor doesn't need yet
   - Creates artificial congestion in the grid
   - Violates the principle of minimal reservation

   Allowed Combinations:
   - CENTER + CENTER: Conservative both ways (minimal reservations)
   - ROUND + ROUND: Aggressive both ways (balanced lookahead)
   - CENTER + ROUND: Conservative reservation, aggressive release (balanced)

   Forbidden Combination:
   - ROUND + CENTER: Aggressive reservation, conservative release (excessive holding)


Q4.5: What are the recommended configuration combinations?
-----------------------------------------------------------
A:
   Recommended Default:
   - reservation_eagerness = CENTER
   - release_eagerness = CENTER
   - reservation_threshold_distance = 0.1
   Rationale: Most predictable, easiest to debug, minimal reservation footprint

   Aggressive Performance:
   - reservation_eagerness = ROUND
   - release_eagerness = ROUND
   - reservation_threshold_distance = 0.25
   Rationale: Smoothest movement, fewer pauses, higher throughput

   Balanced:
   - reservation_eagerness = CENTER
   - release_eagerness = ROUND
   - reservation_threshold_distance = 0.15
   Rationale: Conservative lookahead with efficient cleanup

SECTION 5: EDGE CASES
================================================================================

Q5.1: What if destination is inside actor's current PSC?
---------------------------------------------------------
A: The actor has ARRIVED. Stop all movement.

   Behavior:
   - Target position = destination (within PSC)
   - No additional SC reservations needed
   - Actor may continue moving toward exact destination point within PSC
   - Once within epsilon distance of destination: stop moving completely

   Implementation:
   ```
   psc_contains_destination = is_point_in_subcell(destination, PSC)
   if psc_contains_destination and distance(actor_pos, dest) < 0.01:
       movement_complete = true
       velocity = (0, 0)
   ```


Q5.2: What if all optimal reservations are blocked?
----------------------------------------------------
A: Fall back through priority list until finding available option.

   Process:
   1. Try optimal triangle (diagonal + cardinal) - BLOCKED
   2. Try alternative triangle (different H/V) - BLOCKED
   3. Try pure cardinal direction - BLOCKED
   4. Try perpendicular cardinal - BLOCKED
   5. All options blocked: WAIT

   Wait Behavior:
   - Actor stops at current position
   - Maintains PSC reservation
   - Checks every tick for any option to become available
   - Resumes movement once any path opens

   No Backtracking: Actor never reserves subcells that increase distance to destination
   (unless using a fallback mode - not in this spec).


Q5.3: What if mirror reservation fails?
----------------------------------------
A: Actor cannot continue on optimal triangle boundary. Recalculate from current position.

   Recovery Process:
   1. Actor is at/near boundary of current triangle
   2. Mirror reservation attempt fails (new SC reserved by someone else)
   3. Identify which of currently reserved SCs is closest to destination
   4. Set that SC as temporary "base" position
   5. Apply standard reservation priority (Q2.3) from that base
   6. This usually results in moving toward the closest reserved SC center

   Example:
   - Current triangle (*, *7, *6), trying to reserve mirror *A - FAILS
   - Currently reserved: PSC=*, also hold *7 and *6
   - Check: distance(dest, *7) vs distance(dest, *6)
   - Assume *6 is closer
   - Move toward *6 center (pure vertical movement)
   - Once centered on *6, set *6 as new PSC
   - Release *7 and *
   - Re-evaluate reservation from *6


Q5.4: What if actor position is on exact boundary between subcells?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A: Use consistent tiebreaker rule for subcell assignment.

   Tiebreaker Rules (applied in order):
   1. If actor already has PSC and position is on PSC boundary: stay in current PSC
   2. If position is exactly on boundary: use subcell that is closer to destination
   3. If equidistant to destination: use subcell with lower ID (x + y * cols)

   This ensures deterministic PSC assignment without oscillation.


Q5.5: What if destination changes while actor is moving?
---------------------------------------------------------
A: Immediately recalculate reservations based on new destination. This is the "organic"
   principle in action.

   Process:
   1. Destination updated
   2. Current tick: re-evaluate optimal triangle for NEW destination
      - Is current triangle still optimal for new destination?
        YES: continue current movement
        NO: proceed to step 3
   3. Release reservations that are no longer useful
   4. Attempt new reservations based on new destination direction
   5. Recalculate target position

   Note: Current PSC is never released due to destination change. Only additional
   reservations beyond PSC are affected.


Q5.6: What if actor spawns on grid boundary?
---------------------------------------------
A: Some neighboring subcells won't exist. Only attempt reservations for valid subcells.

   Example: Actor spawns at top-left corner
   - PSC candidates: only SE quadrant subcells exist
   - Neighbors *1, *2, *3, *8 do NOT exist (would be outside grid)
   - Only *4, *5, *6, *7 are valid reservation candidates

   Reservation Logic:
   - Filter priority list (Q2.3) to only include valid (in-bounds) subcells
   - Attempt reservation in filtered priority order
   - If all valid options blocked: wait as usual (Q5.2)


Q5.7: What if multiple actors try to reserve same subcell simultaneously?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A: Subcell reservation must be atomic. Use mutex/lock or actor ID precedence.

   Recommended Implementation: Actor ID Precedence
   ```
   function try_reserve_subcell(actor_id, subcell):
       if subcell.owner == None:
           subcell.owner = actor_id
           return SUCCESS
       elif subcell.owner == actor_id:
           return SUCCESS  # Actor already owns it
       else:
           return BLOCKED  # Another actor owns it
   ```

   Alternative: Priority Queue
   - Actors with lower ID get priority in tiebreak
   - Ensures deterministic resolution of conflicts

   This prevents race conditions in multi-threaded implementations.

SECTION 6: ALGORITHM SUMMARY
================================================================================

Q6.1: What is the complete per-tick algorithm?
-----------------------------------------------

A: Each tick, for each actor, execute this sequence:

```
1. INITIALIZATION CHECK
   if actor has no PSC:
       attempt to reserve any subcell in actor's supercell
       if success:
           set as PSC
       else:
           stay at spawn position, retry next tick
       return  # Done for this tick

2. ARRIVAL CHECK
   if destination is in PSC and distance(actor_pos, dest) < 0.01:
       stop moving (arrived)
       return

3. UPDATE PSC
   determine which subcell contains actor_position
   if different from current PSC:
       verify new subcell is reserved by this actor
       set new subcell as PSC
       apply release_eagerness logic to old PSC

4. RESERVATION ATTEMPT
   if distance(actor_pos, target_pos) <= reservation_threshold_distance:
       calculate destination direction
       determine H vs V preference (Q1.5)

       if currently in optimal triangle approaching boundary:
           attempt mirror reservation (Q2.6)
       else:
           attempt standard reservation priority (Q2.3)

5. TARGET CALCULATION
   if no reservations beyond PSC:
       target = PSC center
   elif pure cardinal reservation:
       target = reserved SC center
   elif optimal triangle reservation:
       target = clamp(destination, triangle_boundary)
   elif non-optimal triangle reservation:
       target = diagonal SC center

6. MOVEMENT
   direction = normalize(target - actor_position)
   displacement = direction * movement_speed * delta_time

   if length(displacement) > distance(actor_pos, target):
       actor_position = target  # Arrived at target
   else:
       actor_position = actor_position + displacement

7. RELEASE LOGIC
   apply release_eagerness rules (Q4.3)
   release subcells that are now redundant
```


Q6.2: How do you test the "organic" principle?
-----------------------------------------------
A: Create position-invariant tests that verify actors behave identically regardless of
   how they arrived at a position.

   Test Template:
   ```
   1. Define: start position S, destination D, grid with obstacles
   2. Run actor from S to D, record trajectory T1
   3. Pick intermediate position P along T1 (e.g., 25%, 50%, 75% of path)
   4. Spawn NEW actor at position P with same destination D
   5. Record trajectory T2 from P to D
   6. Compare: T2 should match the subpath of T1 from P onward
   ```

   Key Assertion:
   ```
   trajectory_from(P, D) should be IDENTICAL regardless of:
   - Whether actor traveled from S->P first
   - Actor's velocity when reaching P
   - Time spent reaching P
   - Previous reservations before reaching P
   ```

   This validates that behavior is purely position-dependent (organic), not
   state-dependent.


Q6.3: What are the key invariants that must always hold?
---------------------------------------------------------
A:
   Invariant 1: PSC Exclusivity
   - Every actor has exactly one PSC
   - No two actors share the same PSC
   - An actor's float position is always within their PSC (or on boundary)

   Invariant 2: Reservation Validity
   - An actor only moves toward positions within reserved subcells
   - Before entering a subcell, it must be reserved by that actor
   - Blocked cells cannot be reserved

   Invariant 3: Progress Toward Destination
   - Reservations never increase distance to destination
   - Actor only releases reservations that are behind or perpendicular to movement
   - (Exception: waiting due to all paths blocked - no reservation change)

   Invariant 4: Finite Reservation Count
   - An actor reserves at most 3 subcells simultaneously:
     * 1 PSC + 2 for optimal triangle
   - (Temporary: may hold 4 during transition with ROUND release, but briefly)

   Invariant 5: Deterministic Behavior
   - Given identical: position, destination, reserved SCs, and grid state
   - Actor must make identical reservation and movement decisions
   - No randomness, no history dependence

SECTION 7: EXAMPLES WALKTHROUGH

Q7.1: Example 1 - Simple diagonal movement NE with H-preference
----------------------------------------------------------------

Setup:
- Actor PSC at (5, 5) subcell (1, 1) [bottom-right quadrant]
- Destination at (8.2, 6.3)
- Distance: dx=3.2, dy=1.3 → |dx| > |dy| → H-preference

Tick 1:
- Actor at (5.4, 5.4) in PSC (5,5,1,1)
- Evaluate direction: NE with H-preference
- Attempt reservation: (E, NE) = (6,5,1,1) and (6,6,1,1)
- Success! Reserve both
- Triangle: ((5,5,1,1), (6,5,1,1), (6,6,1,1))
- Check if optimal: ray to (8.2, 6.3) passes through triangle → YES
- Target: clamp((8.2, 6.3), triangle) = intersection with East edge
- Move toward target

Tick 5:
- Actor at (5.9, 5.6), approaching boundary between (6,5,1,1) and (6,6,1,1)
- Within threshold of target
- Attempt mirror reservation: need (7,6,1,1) to form new triangle
- Success! Reserve (7,6,1,1)
- Release (5,5,1,1) per release_eagerness
- New triangle: ((6,5,1,1), (6,6,1,1), (7,6,1,1))
- New PSC: (6,5,1,1) [actor crossed into it]
- Continue toward destination

Continues until destination reached...


Q7.2: Example 2 - Blocked optimal path, fallback to pure cardinal
------------------------------------------------------------------

Setup:
- Actor PSC at (3, 3) subcell (0, 0) [top-left quadrant]
- Destination at (6.0, 6.0)
- Direction: SE, distance equal in x and y → V-preference

Tick 1:
- Attempt optimal: (S, SE) = (3,4,0,0) and (4,4,0,0)
- (4,4,0,0) is BLOCKED (reserved by another actor)
- Fallback: try alternative (E, SE)
- (4,3,0,0) is FREE, (4,4,0,0) still BLOCKED
- Fallback: try pure S = (3,4,0,0)
- Success! Reserve (3,4,0,0)
- Target: (3,4,0,0) center = (3.25, 4.25)
- Move toward target (pure vertical movement)

Tick 4:
- Actor reaches (3.25, 4.25), now in PSC (3,4,0,0)
- (4,4,0,0) still blocked
- Attempt reservation from new position
- Try (S, SE) from this new PSC
- ...continues with new reservation attempts


Q7.3: Example 3 - Mirror reservation fails, re-route to SC center
------------------------------------------------------------------

Setup:
- Actor in optimal triangle ((5,5,1,1), (5,6,1,1), (6,6,1,1))
- Moving toward destination at (7.5, 8.0)
- Approaching boundary between (5,6,1,1) and (6,6,1,1)

Tick 10:
- Within threshold of boundary
- Attempt mirror reservation: need (6,7,1,1)
- BLOCKED by another actor!
- Current reservations: PSC=(5,5,1,1), also hold (5,6,1,1), (6,6,1,1)
- Determine closest to destination:
  * distance((7.5,8.0), (5,6,1,1)) = ~2.8
  * distance((7.5,8.0), (6,6,1,1)) = ~2.3
- (6,6,1,1) is closer
- New target: (6,6,1,1) center
- Move toward (6,6,1,1) center

Tick 14:
- Reached (6,6,1,1) center, set as new PSC
- Release (5,5,1,1) and (5,6,1,1)
- Re-evaluate reservation from (6,6,1,1)
- (6,7,1,1) now FREE!
- Resume optimal triangle movement

SECTION 9: OPEN QUESTIONS / FUTURE EXTENSIONS
================================================================================

Q9.1: Should actors prioritize avoiding other actors' reserved paths?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Not in current spec. Actors only check if subcell is reserved, not whether it's
on another actor's optimal path. This could be a future enhancement for smoother
multi-actor behavior.


Q9.2: What about dynamic obstacles that appear/disappear?
----------------------------------------------------------
Not addressed in current spec. If an obstacle appears in a reserved subcell,
system behavior is undefined. Suggest: treat as reservation failure, trigger
re-routing.


Q9.3: Should there be a maximum wait time before forcing re-routing?
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Not in current spec. Actor could theoretically wait indefinitely if all paths
blocked. Consider adding timeout with forced alternative destination or backtracking.


Q9.4: How to handle very slow movement speeds or high delta_time?
------------------------------------------------------------------
If displacement per tick exceeds subcell size, actor could "skip" subcells.
Recommend: clamp delta_time to maximum value, or use fixed timestep.
